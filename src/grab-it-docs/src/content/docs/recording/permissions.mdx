Recording uses your screen and, depending on the [options](/grab-it/recording/options/) you pick, your microphone, your camera and your mouse and keyboard. Only macOS asks for permission first; Windows and Linux record straight away.

<Tabs syncKey="os">
<TabItem label="Windows" icon="seti:windows">

Grab-it needs no permission to record. The webcam bubble may use your camera without asking, as it's Grab-it's own window.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="macOS" icon="apple">

Grab-it asks for all four the first time you open it, on its **Set up Grab-it** page:

1. Click **Allow all**. macOS asks about the Microphone and the Camera, then about Screen Recording and Input Monitoring.
2. Turn on **Grab-it** under Screen Recording and Input Monitoring in System Settings: each prompt offers to open it, and so does **Open** on the page. macOS only lets Grab-it use these two after it restarts, so the page shows them as *Turn on in System Settings* until then.
3. Click **Restart Grab-it**. It reopens on its hotkeys, and your first capture works.
4. On macOS 15 and later, macOS then asks whether to let Grab-it *bypass the system private window picker and directly access your screen and audio*. Choose **Allow For One Month**. Grab-it brings this up as it reopens, so it doesn't interrupt your first capture. macOS asks again about once a month, even with Screen Recording on; see [Troubleshooting](/grab-it/reference/troubleshooting/#macos-asks-to-bypass-the-system-private-window-picker).

Each row shows **Allowed**, or **Off** with a way to **Open System Settings**. **Skip for now** closes it. The page comes back each time you open Grab-it only while Screen Recording is off, or to finish setup after **Restart Grab-it**. The Microphone, Camera and Input Monitoring don't bring it back, whether you declined them or were never asked.

Anything you didn't allow is asked for again when it's first needed:

| Permission | Needed for | Without it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Screen Recording** | Every capture and recording. | Grab-it can't capture or record. It offers to open System Settings. |
| **Microphone** | Recording an MP4 with a microphone. The recording waits for your answer. | The recording is saved without your voice, and the card says why. |
| **Camera** | The **Webcam** bubble. | The bubble can't show your camera. |
| **Input Monitoring** | Recording with **Clicks** or **Keys** on. | The recording is saved without clicks or keystrokes, and the card says why. |

System sound needs **Screen Recording**, as it's captured with the screen, and macOS 13 or later.

If you said **Don't Allow** earlier, Grab-it shows a message with **Open System Settings** the next time. To turn a permission on yourself:

1. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.
2. Choose **Screen Recording**, **Microphone**, **Camera** or **Input Monitoring**.
3. Turn on **Grab-it**.
4. For Screen Recording and Input Monitoring, quit and reopen Grab-it if macOS asks you to.

### Automatic scrolling

[Scrolling capture](/grab-it/capture/scrolling/) needs **Accessibility** permission so Grab-it can move the pointer and send scroll events. This is separate from Screen Recording and Input Monitoring. Grab-it checks it before the capture starts without asking: when it is off, the guide offers **Open Settings** and the capture runs by hand.

To allow it later, open **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility** and turn on **Grab-it**. Start a new scrolling capture to use automatic scrolling. Moving the mouse more than 8 points pauses automatic scrolling for the rest of that capture, even when permission is allowed.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Linux" icon="linux">

On X11, Grab-it needs no permission to record, and the webcam bubble may use your camera without asking.

Under Wayland, recording isn't available: only your desktop may read the screen there, and its screenshot picker can't record. See [Troubleshooting](/grab-it/reference/troubleshooting/#on-linux-wayland-the-desktop-asks-me-to-pick-an-area-itself).

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

:::note
Updating from an older version can make macOS treat Grab-it as a new app, once. If recording stops working after an update, turn Grab-it off and on again under **Screen Recording**. See [Troubleshooting](/grab-it/reference/troubleshooting/).
:::
