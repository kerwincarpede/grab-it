Some things vanish the moment you press a key: an open menu, a tooltip, a hover effect. A timed capture waits first, so you have time to set them up.

## From the tray

On Windows and macOS, click the tray icon to open the hub. Click **Timed** and choose a delay.

| Item | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Region in 3 s** | Waits 3 seconds, then freezes the screen for you to drag a region |
| **Region in 5 s** | The same, after 5 seconds |
| **Region in 10 s** | The same, after 10 seconds |
| **Screen in 5 s** | Waits 5 seconds, then captures every monitor |

You can also right-click the tray icon and choose **Timed capture**. On Linux, open the tray menu and choose **Timed capture**.

Start the countdown, open the menu or hover the tooltip you want, and wait. For a region, the screen freezes once the time is up, with your menu still open, and you drag as usual.

## From Capture & record…

The [Capture & record…](/grab-it/capture/all-in-one/) toolbar has a **Timer**. Click it, or press `8`, to step it through **off**, **3 s**, **5 s** and **10 s**. It works with **Region**, **Window** and **Screen**.

This works the other way round: you pick the area *first*, on the frozen screen. The overlay then closes, and once the time is up Grab-it captures that same area **as the screen looks then**. So you can choose exactly what to capture, then open the menu inside it.

## The countdown

While a timed capture waits, from the tray, the hub's **Timed** items or the Capture & record… **Timer**, a small pill at the top of the screen under the pointer counts down the seconds left, next to **Capturing…**. Click **Cancel** on it to stop the capture from starting.

The pill closes just before the capture is taken, so it is never in your screenshot or on the frozen screen. Starting another timed capture while one is counting down replaces it.
